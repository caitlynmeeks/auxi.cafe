Abstract

Can a continuous-state reservoir be compiled into a genuine finite-state automaton at negligible cost? The lab had already asked this once with the obvious tool — quantizing every coordinate of the state to b bits — and the answer was a clean no: determinism never appeared at any resolution from 1 to 23 bits, because a coarser grid still leaves astronomically many representable states. This study changes one thing: instead of rounding coordinates independently, the state is snapped, every step, to its nearest vector in a fixed codebook of K states the pond has actually visited — a hard cap on the cardinality of the whole state vector. That one change flips the answer. (1) At every tested K ∈ {16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096}, on both seeds — 10 of 10 cells — the pond freezes into a real automaton: identical recent-character suffixes of depth 7–11 produce the identical state at every one of 40,000 probed positions. (2) The dial is smooth: prediction cost degrades monotonically as the codebook shrinks, 8/8 adjacent comparisons, no cliff, no reversal. (3) The largest tested lattice is nearly free: K=4096 costs only 0.053–0.057 bits over the unquantized float32 reference — the cheapest tested point on the dial is also the one that compiles.

Keywords reservoir computing · vector quantization · finite-state automata · state-space discretization · determinism · pre-registration

§1 The question

A reservoir's state is a point in a 1024-dimensional continuum; its dynamics are checkable only statistically. A finite-state machine, by contrast, can be tabulated, audited, and proven things about. The lab's earlier crystal-pond study (run ledger #7) posed the compilation question with per-coordinate rounding and got a striking negative: bits-per-character cost melted smoothly below b≈6 bits and was exact to float32 from b=8 — but short-memory determinism stayed at exactly 0.0 at every b from 1 to 23. Rounding re-injects a small perturbation each step, and a b-bit grid over 1024 coordinates still has vastly more representable states than any run visits; the pond never repeated itself in 40,000 sampled states. The goat-family study of descent trails (Goat Descent) had meanwhile left an unbuilt image in the lab's notes: a pond whose state walks a fixed lattice of footholds. This study builds that pond, asking: is a hard cardinality cap — not a finer or coarser grid — what forces a reservoir to become a machine?

§2 The machine

The base pond is the house champion tanh reservoir at its best measured operating point (N=1024, ρ=0.8, sparse fanin-10 mixing, house input map, leak 1.0) [3] — the same anchor cell the crystal-pond study quantized. One line is added to its update:

# C = codebook: K states the unquantized pond visited
#     during a fixed 100k-char warmup, sampled once,
#     frozen for the whole run (no k-means, no synthesis)
x ← argmin_{c ∈ C} ‖x − c‖   # after every update step

The snapped state feeds the next step, so the closed-loop dynamics live entirely on the K footholds — nearest-neighbor vector quantization [1] of the state, with the codebook drawn from the pond's own visited set rather than fit to it. K sweeps {16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096}. Instruments are copied verbatim from the crystal-pond study for direct comparability: ridge and logistic validation bpc on standard text8 splits [4] (2M train characters), and the determinism probe (T5 in the lab's internal instrument numbering): over 40,000 sampled positions, group states by their length-k character suffix and ask whether every multi-sample suffix class lands in exactly one state. k* is the smallest depth at which that fraction reaches 1.0, judged only where at least 20 multi-sample classes support it — at K=4096 (seed 0), k*=10 rests on 2,172 such classes, not a thin slice. A contract cell (K=∞, no snapping) reproduced the plain unquantized pond bit-for-bit through the same code path in both pre-registration smokes.

§3 What we registered in advance

  • M1 (existence, per K, judged independently): does k* come out finite at each of the five K's? Named alternative, per K: the snap-to-nearest correction re-injects noise the way rounding did, and a small cardinality cap does not force determinism by itself. Any K freezing is newsworthy against the 0/23 contrast.
  • M2 (the dial): logistic val bpc strictly improves along the whole ladder K = 16 → 4096, all four adjacent pairs (tie band ±0.005). Named alternative: non-monotone — disclosed honestly, the pre-registration smoke had hinted k* might dip at K=256, so cost might too.
  • MQ1 (the price): logistic val bpc at K=4096 lands in [2.6519, 2.80] — within 0.15 bits of the crystal-pond float32 reference (2.6519; quoted, not recomputed, and structurally not this dial's K→∞ limit, since a codebook pond only revisits stored anchors).

The registration also pre-stated the propagation rule: M1 firing at any K is provisional-only on a first seed; M1 plus MQ1 landing at the same K — a checkable automaton at negligible cost, the crystal-pond study's own named target — is EUREKA-tier under the lab's constitution, and triggers a replication queued ahead of everything else before any entry in the findings book. That is what happened. The seed-1 rerun registered the same three claims (N1/N2/NQ1), each with its own unbundled promotion rule.

M1/N1: 10 of 10 cells froze. Every lattice size, on both seeds, reached full determinism within a suffix depth of 7–11 characters — k*'s exact value is not seed-stable, but its finiteness is universal, always well inside the probe's depth limit of 20. The contrast with per-coordinate rounding is not a near-miss; it is total (0/23 there, 10/10 here). Note the freeze is not a collapse into a trivial handful of states: at K=4096, over 3,300 distinct codewords remain in active use.

M2/N2: the dial is smooth, 8/8. Logistic val bpc improves strictly at every adjacent step of the ladder on both seeds, with gaps (0.385/0.279/0.272/0.131 at seed 0; 0.403/0.259/0.271/0.133 at seed 1) tens of times the tie band. The disclosed non-monotone worry did not materialize for cost, even though k* itself is mildly non-monotone in K at seed 0 (the two curves are not the same shape — noted in the journal, not chased).

MQ1/NQ1: the cheap end compiles. K=4096 sits 0.0570 bits (seed 0) and 0.0531 bits (seed 1) above the float32 logistic reference — a third of the registered tolerance — while still freezing at k*=10/11. Ridge tells the same story (3.1406/3.1403 against the reference's 3.1073), so the price is not an artifact of the trained instrument. Both halves of the win-win landed at the same K on both seeds; per the pre-stated rule, all three claims promoted unbundled after the second pass.

§5 What it means

Cardinality, not resolution, is what compiles. Per-coordinate rounding makes the state grid coarser but leaves the reachable set effectively unbounded, and the rounding perturbation keeps the crystal from freezing. A fixed codebook of visited states caps the reachable set at K outright — and at every tested K the closed-loop dynamics promptly become a function of a short window of recent input. The result rhymes with the automata-extraction literature, where finite abstractions of trained RNNs are constructed by external algorithms [2]; here the network is the automaton, exactly, by construction, and the question was only what that costs.

What it costs is measured, small, and smooth. The compiled pond at K=4096 gives up about a twentieth of a bit of next-character prediction relative to its continuous self, and the price rises predictably — no cliff — as the foothold list shrinks toward 16. For the lab's program, this makes "a real, inspectable finite-state machine" an artifact with a measured price, rather than a hoped-for limit.

Open, and posted honestly. Why does actually visited matter, as against an arbitrary codebook of the same size? A random-codebook control is under registered study in the lab's journal and remains unpromoted; under house rules it stays out of print, and this paper makes no claim about it. Also open: how far the near-free regime extends past K=4096, and whether the compiled automaton transfers across ponds.
